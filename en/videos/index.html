How to watch test videos

What test videos show, and what still has to be measured on site

This page has 25 manufacturer videos: forklifts hitting barriers, water poured on anti-slip tape, lights switched off to test photoluminescent tape, plus 3D animations.

Real footage is not the same as 3D animation

A 3D animation shows what a product is designed to do: the rail flexes on impact and comes back, the gate closes by itself after someone walks through. Real footage shows one specific test, with one vehicle, one barrier size, on one floor.

Do not treat an animation as proof of real results. And do not treat one test as the result for every barrier size.

What to look at in a forklift impact clip

Watch each clip twice. First watch the impact. Then watch only the base.

  • Vehicle: diesel or electric forklift, loaded or empty
  • Direction: a square hit or a glancing blow
  • Impact point: the forks, the vehicle body or the load
  • Barrier size: 1, 2 or 3 rails, light or heavy duty
  • After the hit: does the rail return straight, do the base and bolts shift

What the videos cannot answer

This website does not state the load or speed of the vehicle in the clips, and does not publish joule figures or impact ratings without a numbered test report. So a video shows how a product takes a hit, not which barrier size suits the vehicles in your warehouse. A clip also cannot tell you:

  • Whether your warehouse floor will hold the barrier base bolts
  • Whether anti-slip tape will grip on the steel stairs or oily floors in your plant
  • How marking tape wears after months on a corner where forklifts turn
  • How long photoluminescent tape glows on your stairs, since that depends on the light that charged it beforehand

Why an on-site survey is still needed

Barrier size, base type and tape type are settled by things you only see standing in the warehouse: the heaviest vehicle on that stretch, how drivers habitually take the corner, the clearance between racks and the lane, floor thickness and condition, and which spots get wet. The surveyor measures these during a running shift. Watch the videos first to know what to ask, then let the survey answer it for your warehouse. For a suggestion before you book, try the quick selector.

Using the videos with your team

Show the tape application clip to your maintenance team before a trial application. Show the forklift impact clips at a safety meeting when discussing replacing steel barriers at rack ends, together with the steel or polymer article. If a clip looks like a spot in your warehouse, note its title and send it with photos through the survey booking page.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about the test videos

See all questions
Who filmed the videos on this page?

The videos and 3D animations were filmed and supplied by the manufacturers, and Thien Khoi has permission to use them on this website. Phone numbers on vehicles are blurred in some clips. Clips showing customer logos, certification marks or joule figures are left out. See the products in the clips under polymer barriers and floor tape.

Can I use a video to choose a barrier size?

Better not. A video shows one test with one vehicle and one barrier size, and this website does not publish the vehicle's load or speed in that test. Barrier size depends on the heaviest vehicle on your stretch, the impact point and the floor, measured on site. Use the quick selector for a starting suggestion.

Were any videos filmed at Thien Khoi projects?

No. The videos on this page were filmed by the manufacturers. Thien Khoi project videos and photos are published only with the client's written consent, with before and after photos on the news page. Thien Khoi does not stage scenes and call them projects, and does not present manufacturer footage as a client story.

Can we use these videos in our plant's safety training?

Playing them straight from this page is simplest: click a clip to watch full screen with sound. The videos belong to the manufacturers, so if you want to use the files elsewhere, such as internal training material, ask Thien Khoi first through the contact page. Show them together with the steel or polymer article so viewers understand the barrier's limits.

Does the clip of water on anti-slip tape mean the tape works on oily floors?

You cannot draw that conclusion. The clip shows the tape still gives grip when its surface is wet with water. Oily floors are a different matter: the oil must be removed before application or the tape will not stick. Spots that are regularly oily need an on-site look to choose the tape type and a cleaning routine. See anti-slip tape.
