How to choose barriers and floor tape

How to choose barriers and floor tape when you check the warehouse yourself

Choosing barriers and floor tape starts with walking at the right time, looking at the right places and writing down enough detail. We explain the questions in the quick selector and how to use the checklist so you have photos ready to send.

Preparing for a factory safety self-check

A self-check is only useful if you walk while the warehouse is running. When it is quiet, every aisle looks safe.

  • Walk during a running shift, ideally at the peak of receiving or dispatch
  • Bring a phone for photos and a tape measure or laser measure
  • Print the floor plan if you have one and number the zones so your notes match
  • Take someone from the forklift team with you. They know where hits happen and photos do not show it
  • If the warehouse runs two shifts, walk once more in the evening

Choosing barriers by vehicle type

The vehicle type decides the barrier size more than the length of the stretch does. When you take notes, write down the heaviest vehicle that has ever run through that stretch, not the one that runs most often. One diesel forklift passing once a week is enough to wreck a barrier chosen for hand pallet trucks.

  • Note the load and usual speed of the vehicle, if you know them
  • Note the impact point: forks, the pallet being carried or the vehicle body
  • Note the gap between the vehicle lane and where the barrier would go
  • If trucks reverse into a dock, note that separately, because that spot needs wheel stops or bollards at the dock edge

Floor line or barrier

Ask this: if the driver loses focus for one second, what will the vehicle touch. If it only drifts over a line and nobody is standing there, a line is enough. If it could touch a person, a loaded rack upright or an electrical panel, you need a hard obstacle. When in doubt, choose a barrier where people are and a line where there are only goods.

Using the warehouse safety checklist so it gives results

The 10-point checklist shows which spots to photograph and send. It does not grade your warehouse as good or bad. Each ticked item should have two photos and one line of notes.

  • Wide photo: stand back so the vehicle lane, walkway, rack or stairs are in one frame
  • Close photo: the dent, forklift paint left on the surface, the worn nosing
  • Note: estimated length, vehicle type, busiest hours

What the quick selector cannot do

The quick selector and the checklist do not replace a site survey. They cannot measure floor thickness, the real gap between rack and vehicle lane, or the corner a given driver habitually takes. Once you have photos, send them to Thien Khoi to get a suggested polymer barrier series or floor tape type for each spot.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about choosing barriers and floor tape

See all questions
What do I need to prepare for a warehouse self-check?

A phone for photos, a tape measure or laser measure, the floor plan if you have one, and a printed 10-point checklist. Walk while the shift is running, with someone from the forklift team, because they know where hits happen. If the warehouse runs two shifts, walk once more in the evening. The printable version is on the resources page.

How do I choose a barrier by forklift type?

Use the heaviest vehicle that has ever run through that stretch as the standard, not the one that runs most often. Hand pallet trucks need a small size, and diesel forklifts carrying tall pallets need a large size and a firm base. Also note the speed and the impact point: forks, pallet or vehicle body. The exact size is set when we measure on site. See the polymer barrier series.

Choosing barriers and floor tape: when is tape enough and when do I need a barrier?

Tape is enough when the job is only to remind: side aisles, pallet zones, zoning by 5S. You need a barrier where people walk close to a vehicle lane, a rack upright is loaded or an electrical panel sits on a corner where trucks turn. Many stretches use both: the barrier blocks, the line shows the way. See floor tape and the three levels of separating people from forklifts.

Can the checklist replace a professional survey?

No. The checklist is only the first step in choosing barriers and floor tape. It shows you which spots to photograph and send, but it cannot measure floor thickness, the real gap between rack and vehicle lane, or the vehicle load. That needs someone on site during a shift. Use the checklist first, then book a survey with the photos you took. See also the 7 zones in a warehouse.

What photos should I send for Thien Khoi to suggest something?

For each spot, one wide photo that shows the lane, rack or stairs in the same frame, and one close photo of the dent, the wear or the slippery spot. Add the estimated length, the vehicle type and the busiest time. Send them through the survey request page or WhatsApp or Zalo. If you are not sure what to photograph, see the 7 zones in a warehouse.
