Floor marking in a factory: paint or tape

No method wins in every zone. Paint and tape both have limits, and the place where one works well is often where the other is weak. Below are the limits of both and how to choose zone by zone.

Paint or tape: the short answer

Choose paint or tape for each zone separately. Tape suits zones that cannot stop for long, zones where the layout changes often, and floors that are dry and clean. Paint suits zones that can stop while it dries and where lines stay fixed for a long time.

The limits of floor paint

Paint bonds to the floor as a coating, with no edge for a wheel to pull up. But paint has limits that a warehouse manager notices as soon as the work is scheduled.

  • Drying time: a freshly painted zone must be closed until the paint is dry, as stated by the paint manufacturer. Vehicles and people have to go around.
  • Smell: many paints smell during application and while drying. Enclosed zones and zones next to offices need ventilation and a suitable work schedule.
  • Zone shutdown: repainting even one stretch of line means cleaning, painting and waiting for it to dry. A plant that runs nonstop rarely has that window.
  • Wear under wheels: paint lines at corners and in front of warehouse doors fade, then have to be repainted.

The limits of floor tape

Once tape is down, you can drive and walk over it. There is no drying time and no painting step, so no smell to wait out. That is why many warehouses choose tape. But tape has weak spots too, and you should know them before you order.

  • Edges lift where forklifts turn: a forklift wheel that pivots on the edge of the tape pulls the edge up. Where trucks turn all day, tape wears and lifts first.
  • Damp floors: moisture rising from the concrete, or mop water trapped under the tape, makes the adhesive lose its grip.
  • Dust: cement dust, cargo dust and oil on the floor form a layer between the adhesive and the floor. If the floor is not cleaned well, the tape lifts from the edges.
  • Tape does not hold a forklift back. It only reminds, just like a painted line.
Do this now: Where people and forklifts pass close together, you need a hard barrier. No line is enough.

Choosing zone by zone in the factory

Go through each zone and ask three questions: can this zone stop while paint dries, do trucks turn on the line, and is the floor dry and clean.

  • Main aisles, rack areas, production areas that are running: tape usually suits, because the zone does not have to close for long.
  • Corners, in front of warehouse doors, spots where trucks turn: both wear out. Sometimes the answer is to move the line out of the wheel track, not to change the material.
  • New buildings, a plant not yet running, lines meant to stay for years: paint suits, because there is time to let it dry.
  • Zones where the layout changes by season, dock areas that stage cargo by order: tape is easier to change.
  • Damp floors, floors that are often oily: treat the floor first. Until you do, both paint and tape will lift.
Do this now: Many plants use both: paint for the fixed main forklift routes, tape for pallet bays, hand pallet truck parking and 5S zones that change often. For the color of each type of line, see the floor marking color chart by 5S practice.

A case: a warehouse that never stops, faded paint lines

Say a warehouse runs three shifts. The paint line for the walkway at the corner and in front of the warehouse door has faded, and new staff can no longer see the route. Repainting would mean closing the corner, and the corner is on the main forklift route.

In this warehouse, apply tape to the straight sections of the main walkway, a short section at a time so forklifts keep running. At the corner, look at the wheel track first. If trucks cut across the line every time they take the corner, move the line or add a barrier at the edge of the walkway. If you change the material but not the forklift route, the new line will wear out in the same place. How to plan the walking route and the forklift route is in the article on separating pedestrians and forklifts.

How to apply tape so it sticks

Most tape lifting starts with floor preparation. The basic steps:

  • Clean off dust, oil and water. Let the floor dry fully.
  • Peel the backing slowly and press from the middle out to the edges.
  • Roll over the tape with a roller after applying so it bonds firmly to the floor.
  • On cold days: the manufacturer's guide for applying anti-slip tape says that below 20°C you should warm the tape with a hair dryer, then roll it with a rubber roller to push out air bubbles.
  • How long tape lasts depends on traffic and wheel type; where forklifts turn all day, consider paint or a more abrasion-resistant tape.
Do this now: The types of tape and how to apply them are on the floor safety tape page.

Do this now: check the lines at the corners

Look at the walkway lines at the corners and in front of the warehouse doors. Wheels wear those out first. Take a photo of each spot and note whether that zone can stop while paint dries.

FAQ

Is floor tape more durable than paint?

There is no general answer. On a straight aisle with a dry, clean floor, tape works well and the zone does not have to stop. Where forklifts turn all day, tape edges lift easily and paint lines fade. Real durability depends on the floor, the vehicles and the application.

How do we prepare a concrete floor for tape?

The floor must be free of dust, oil and water, and fully dry. Peel the backing slowly, press from the middle to the edges, then roll it. If the floor is damp or often oily, treat the floor first, or the tape will lift from the edges.

Do we have to stop the factory to mark lines with paint?

You must close the zone being painted until the paint is dry, as stated by the paint manufacturer. Plants that run nonstop usually split the work into short sections and do it when traffic is low.

Should we use both paint and tape in one factory?

You can. Many plants paint the fixed main forklift routes and use tape for pallet bays, hand pallet truck parking and zones that change often. The whole plant still needs one color chart.

Can floor lines replace a barrier?

No. A line only reminds. It does not hold back a person or a vehicle. Where people and forklifts pass close together you need a hard barrier, see polymer barriers.
