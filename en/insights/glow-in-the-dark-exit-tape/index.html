Glow in the dark tape for exits: where to apply it so the way out still shows in a power cut

Glow in the dark tape for exits only glows if the lights have charged it first. In the right spots, under the right lights, it redraws the way out when the power fails. Put it in a corner that is dark all day and it has nothing to give in a power cut.

What is glow in the dark exit tape, and where does it go?

Glow in the dark tape for exits is adhesive tape with a photoluminescent layer: it absorbs light while the lights are on, then glows on its own and fades when the power fails. Apply it in 5 places: low along the wall, step edges, exit door frames, handrails and obstacles. Each spot needs enough light every day.

It does not replace emergency lighting or the exit signs and lights required by regulation. A 2011 UL document on luminous egress path marking says these systems are used with exit signs to supplement the required emergency lighting. Emergency lighting is the main layer and glow tape is the last backup.

How glow in the dark tape works: charge, glow, fade

When lamps or daylight shine on the tape, the photoluminescent layer stores energy. A spot that never gets light has nothing to give back.

Switch the lights off and the tape releases that energy as light, usually a pale green. There is no wiring or battery, so there is nothing electrical to fail.

That light is brightest right after the lights go off, then dims. How long it lasts depends on the tape type and how much light it absorbed, so there is no single figure. Hours printed on a pack only compare when the test conditions come with them: what light, for how long, measured after how many minutes. When choosing tape, ask the supplier for the test method, for example Germany's DIN 67510-1.

Glow in the dark vs reflective tape: what is the difference?

The two get mixed up. Reflective tape does not glow on its own: it sends back the light shining on it, so a forklift driver behind the headlights sees it most clearly. Photoluminescent tape glows by itself after charging, so you can still see it when every light is out.

Where vehicle lights reach, use reflective tape. Where people must see the way in a power cut, use photoluminescent tape. Stripe colors follow your plant's floor marking color chart.

Where should glow tape go on an exit route?

Night shift, and the power cuts out while a picker is at the far end of an aisle. Between two tall racks there is no door in sight. At the junction the closed dock door is straight ahead and the fire exit is in the other corner. A glow strip low along the wall and an arrow at the junction keep that person from heading for the dock.

UL lists the features to mark: doors, door hardware and frames, stairs, landings, handrails, obstacles and egress symbols. The guidance strip should be essentially continuous, broken only at doorways and where another corridor crosses. In a warehouse that comes down to 5 places:

  • Low along the wall: a continuous strip near the floor, with arrows at aisle junctions.
  • Step edges: the edge of every step up to the mezzanine or office, and the landing edges. On wet steps, ask about anti-slip tape with a glow edge.
  • Exit doors: outline the frame and mark the push bar or handle.
  • Handrails: a strip along the rail, especially on steel stairs up to a mezzanine.
  • Obstacles on the way out: rack feet sticking out, columns, steps at the dock door, kick rail edges. Mark them at foot height.

How much light does the tape need to charge?

The tape only gives back the light it took in. According to UL, the charging light need not be emergency lighting, but it must be on whenever the building is occupied, and timers or sensors must not leave the tape without a charging source for long periods. Instructions for UL listed systems state the minimum amount and type of light.

The same document says that to be listed under UL 1994, a photoluminescent system must still perform adequately 90 minutes after being charged with only 1 foot-candle (about 10.8 lux) for no more than 60 minutes. Treat that as the test condition for a UL listing; it does not tell you how long a given roll will glow.

  • Stairs with motion sensor lights: ask the tape maker whether that charges it enough, or keep the lights on for the whole shift.
  • Do not apply tape in corners that are dark all day: behind pallet stacks, under a mezzanine, behind a door that stays open.
  • After changing lamps, adding racks or moving the layout, check that light still reaches the tape.

Which standards cover photoluminescent wayfinding?

In Vietnam, emergency lighting and exit signs follow the current fire safety (PCCC) regulations; check with your fire safety consultant. The documents below are international references, not Vietnamese law. This article does not say any tape meets them.

  • ISO 16069 (2017 edition): safety way guidance systems (SWGS). General principles for both electrically powered and phosphorescent components, including choice of material, layout, installation and maintenance. Adopted in Germany as DIN ISO 16069:2019.
  • DIN 67510 (Germany): Part 1, 2020 edition, covers measuring and marking phosphorescent products at the producer. Part 3, on low location guidance systems, is withdrawn; DIN Media recommends DIN ISO 16069 instead.
  • UL 1994 (US): safety standard for luminous egress path marking systems, both electrically powered and photoluminescent.
  • OSHA 1910.37 (US): exit routes must be lit well enough to see along them and kept free and unobstructed, with no materials or equipment placed in them, even temporarily.

How do you clean and check glow tape?

Dust, oil and tire marks on the surface cut how much light the tape absorbs and how bright it glows. Wipe it with a damp cloth following the manufacturer's instructions, with no wire brushes or unknown solvents. Once a month, run this check:

  • At the end of a shift, switch off the stair and exit route lights. Stand at the point farthest from the exit and look along the route.
  • Follow the glow strip to the door and note any gaps, sections much dimmer than the rest, or anything covering it.
  • Lights back on: clean dirty sections, cut out peeling or torn ones and apply new tape.
  • Clear pallets, trolleys and boxes left on the exit route.
  • Log the date, who checked and what was fixed in the plant's safety log. Check again after repainting, lamp changes or rack moves.

Do it now: switch off the stair lights for 30 seconds

Tonight, at the end of the shift, switch off the stair and exit route lights for 30 seconds. Stand at the point farthest from the door. If you cannot see the step edges or the way to the exit, that spot needs glow tape, or the lights there are not charging the tape you already have.

FAQ

How long does glow in the dark tape glow?

There is no single figure. The tape is brightest right after the lights go off and then fades; how long depends on the tape type and how much light it absorbed. When comparing two tapes, ask for the test conditions behind the number. The surest check is to switch the lights off where the tape is applied.

Can glow tape replace exit signs or emergency lighting?

No. It is the last backup layer, used together with the emergency lighting and the exit signs and lights required by regulation. UL also describes egress path marking as a supplement to required emergency lighting. Check the current fire safety regulations for each item.

Why does the tape not glow when the lights go off?

Most often it never charged: it sits in a dark corner, behind racks, or under sensor lights that stay off for long periods. Next come dust, oil or paint over the surface. Sometimes the tape on that spot is reflective, which only shines when a light hits it. Check the lights, clean the tape, then test again in the dark.

Is glow tape on step edges a trip hazard?

Flat tape with sealed edges is less likely to catch a foot. Peeling edges do. In the plant, apply it on a clean, dry surface, check the edges monthly and replace peeling sections at once.

How often should glow tape be checked?

We suggest once a month: switch the lights off at the end of a shift and follow the glow strip to the door. Check again after repainting, lamp changes or layout changes. Clean dirty sections, replace peeling, torn or clearly dimmer ones, and record it in the safety log.
