Home / Floor safety tape

Clear lanes. No more slippery steps. Exits you can see in the dark.

Four tape types for four different jobs on the shop floor: floor marking, anti-slip, reflective, photoluminescent.

See the 4 tape typesFloor marking color chart
Photo: manufacturer

PVC floor marking tape

Separate people and forklifts, mark pallet zones, fire cabinets and pallet truck parking. Many colors for 5S zoning.

  • Ready to use once applied. No waiting for paint to dry, no long shutdown
  • Suits main aisles, racking areas, production areas
  • Wears faster where forklifts turn all day: check on site before choosing
Do this now: Look at the walkway lines on corners and in front of warehouse doors. That is where wheels wear the lines away first.
Photo: manufacturer

Anti-slip tape

Gritty surface for step edges, ramps, wet floors, steel checker-plate steps and machine platforms.

  • Step edges on stairs to offices and mezzanines
  • Ramps to warehouse doors, spots that get rainwater
  • Holds firmly only on a clean, dry surface, applied per the manufacturer's instructions
Do this now: Shine a flashlight along the step edges. Wherever they are worn smooth, tape there first.
Photo: manufacturer

Reflective tape

Lights up when light hits it. Good for columns on corners, bollards, basement entrances, parking areas.

  • Wrap column corners on forklift lanes
  • Wrap bollards in parking areas and at gates
  • Needs a light source shining on it. In total darkness, use photoluminescent tape
Do this now: On the night shift, stand where forklifts turn. Any column lost in the dark needs wrapping.
Photo: manufacturer

Photoluminescent (glow-in-the-dark) tape

Absorbs light while the lights are on. Glows on its own for a while after they go off. No power, no wiring, no switch.

  • Step edges on stairs to offices and mezzanines
  • Routes from production areas to emergency exits
  • Does not replace the emergency lighting or exit signs required by regulation. It is the last backup layer
Do this now: Tonight, switch off the stair lights for 30 seconds. If you cannot see the step edges, you know what to do.

Floor marking color chart

One color, one meaning. The whole plant reads it the same way.

A reference chart based on common 5S practice. Each plant should settle on its own chart and post it at the shop floor entrance.

YellowWalkways, lanes for forklifts and people
WhitePositions for equipment, carts, tables
Yellow and blackImpact and trip hazards, dock edges
Red and whiteKeep clear: in front of electrical panels, fire extinguishers
RedFire equipment, defective goods, scrap
GreenFirst aid, finished goods, passed goods
BlueRaw materials, work in progress
OrangeGoods on hold, waiting for inspection or a decision
Source pendingThe color chart needs a source (a standard or guidance document, with access date) before it goes on the live site.

Dust, oil or water under the tape is where it peels first.

Apply and press down

Peel the liner slowly. Press from the center out to the edges.

Roll it

Roll over the tape after applying so it bonds firmly to the floor.
