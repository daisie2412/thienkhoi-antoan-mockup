PVC floor marking tape

Separate people and forklifts, mark pallet zones, fire cabinets and pallet truck parking. Many colors for 5S zoning.

  • Ready to use once applied, without waiting for paint to dry or a long shutdown
  • Suits main aisles, racking areas, production areas
  • Wears faster where forklifts turn all day: check on site before choosing
Do this now: Look at the walkway lines on corners and in front of warehouse doors. That is where wheels wear the lines away first.
Photo: manufacturer

Anti-slip tape

Gritty surface for step edges, ramps, wet floors, steel checker-plate steps and machine platforms.

  • Step edges on stairs to offices and mezzanines
  • Ramps to warehouse doors, spots that get rainwater
  • Holds firmly only on a clean, dry surface, applied per the manufacturer's instructions
Do this now: Shine a flashlight along the step edges. Wherever they are worn smooth, tape there first.
Photo: manufacturer

Reflective tape

Lights up when light hits it. Good for columns on corners, bollards, basement entrances, parking areas.

  • Wrap column corners on forklift lanes
  • Wrap bollards in parking areas and at gates
  • Needs a light source shining on it. In total darkness, use photoluminescent tape
Do this now: On the night shift, stand where forklifts turn. Any column lost in the dark needs wrapping.
Photo: manufacturer

Photoluminescent (glow-in-the-dark) tape

Absorbs light while the lights are on and glows on its own for a while after they go off. It needs no power, wiring or switch.

  • Step edges on stairs to offices and mezzanines
  • Routes from production areas to emergency exits
  • Does not replace the emergency lighting or exit signs required by regulation. It is the last backup layer
Do this now: Tonight, switch off the stair lights for 30 seconds. If you cannot see the step edges, they need photoluminescent tape.
Photo: manufacturer

Floor safety tape

Floor safety tape: choose by floor, apply it so it sticks, know the limits

Floor safety tape lasts only when the type suits the floor and it is applied properly. The sections below cover how to choose by floor surface and traffic, the application steps from the manufacturer's guide, and what tape cannot do.

Choose by floor surface and traffic

The same roll of floor marking tape can grip firmly on clean epoxy and peel early on dusty bare concrete. Before choosing, look at three things: what the floor surface is, whether the floor is often wet or oily, and which vehicles cross the line.

  • Epoxy or paint in good condition: wipe off oil and you can apply
  • Bare concrete: vacuum, wipe, let it dry fully. Crumbly or pitted concrete needs treatment first
  • Where forklift wheels turn on the spot: the tape edge gets pulled hardest, so consider moving the line or using paint at that exact point
  • Ramps to the warehouse door, floors outside under a canopy: exposed to sun and rain, so ask for the outdoor specification of each type before choosing

Four types of tape and common mix-ups

The usual mistake is using the wrong type of tape. Reflective tape on stairs with no light shining on it does not glow. Glow-in-the-dark tape in a corner where the light is off all day never charges, so in a power cut it has nothing to give.

Anti-slip stair tape belongs on the nosing, where the foot lands first and the surface wears smooth first. Reflective tape and exit glow tape can share one zone: reflective for columns on forklift routes where headlights reach, glow for step edges and the way out.

Applying it so it sticks

Tape usually starts peeling at the edges and at the start of a run. From the manufacturer's application guide:

  • Peel the liner back a short section at a time, pressing the tape down as you go. Do not peel a long run and then lay it
  • Below 20°C, warm the tape surface with a hair dryer
  • Roll with a rubber roller after laying to push out air bubbles, especially along both edges

Floor tape or floor paint

Tape can be walked on right after it is laid, and when the layout changes you lift it and lay it again. Paint suits a new floor, a large area and a layout that rarely changes, but it needs drying time and that zone has to stop. Many plants use both: paint for fixed main lanes, tape for areas that change, such as pallet staging. The article on floor paint or floor tape compares them in more detail and states the limits of each.

Limits of tape

  • A line on the floor does not stop a vehicle. Where people walk close to a forklift lane you still need a pedestrian barrier
  • Glow-in-the-dark tape needs enough light for long enough to charge before a power cut
  • Floor marking colors are common practice, and we have found no general mandatory rule. See the factory floor marking color chart
  • Glow tape is brightest right after the lights go out, then fades, so exit routes need enough light each day for the tape to charge.
  • Areas that change layout often should use tape that comes off cleanly, so re-marking does not mean scraping adhesive off the floor.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about floor safety tape

See all questions
Should we mark lines with paint or tape?

It depends on the spot. Floor tape can be used right after laying, so it suits areas that change layout, such as pallet staging, and places that cannot stop for long. Paint suits a new floor, a large area and a layout that rarely changes. Many plants use both. The article on floor paint or floor tape compares them in detail and states the limits of each.

Can floor tape be used on lanes with heavy forklift traffic?

Yes, on stretches where vehicles run straight. Where forklift wheels turn on the spot, brake hard or keep turning around, the tape edge is pulled hardest and wears first. At those points we need to see the spot to decide whether to move the line, change the tape or paint that exact point. Send photos of the corner through the survey request page, or see the forklift lane zone.

What preparation does bare concrete need before taping?

Vacuum, wipe off oil and let the floor dry fully. Crumbly or pitted concrete needs treatment first, because tape only holds when the floor surface is firm. When applying, peel the liner in short sections, pressing as you go, then roll with a rubber roller to push out air bubbles. In cold weather, warm the tape surface as the manufacturer's guide says. See the tape application video.

Does anti-slip tape stick to checker plate steps?

Yes, if the steel is free of oil and fully dry before taping. Checker plate steps in a factory often carry machine oil, so degrease thoroughly, let it dry, then tape and roll the tape so it hugs the step. For platforms and machine bases there are also separate anti-slip plates. See anti-slip tape and the stairs zone.

How long does glow-in-the-dark tape glow in a power cut?

The tape is brightest right after the lights go out, then fades. How long it glows depends on the tape type and how much light it absorbed beforehand, so exit routes need enough light every day. During the survey Thien Khoi checks the lighting on your exit routes before recommending a tape, see glow-in-the-dark tape.

Is there a mandatory rule for floor marking colors?

We have found no general mandatory rule on floor marking colors for factories in Vietnam. The color chart in use is common 5S practice. Each plant sets its own in its internal rules, so that everyone in the plant reads the colors the same way. See the floor marking color chart and the printable version on the resources page.

When we change the plant layout, does lifting the tape leave adhesive?

It depends on the tape type, the floor and how long the tape has been down. We do not give a general conclusion without the manufacturer's removal specification. Before changing a layout, try lifting a short piece in a hidden corner. If your warehouse changes layout often, tell us before you book a survey. See also the article on floor paint or floor tape.
